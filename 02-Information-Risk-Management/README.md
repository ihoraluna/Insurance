# Information Risk Management

A concise study reference covering the fundamentals of Information Risk Management (IRM) — including key concepts, processes, best practices, frameworks, and the role of IRM in protecting organizational information and managing information security risks.

## 📌 Contents

- **What is Information Risk Management?**
  - Systematic process to identify, analyze, evaluate, treat, monitor, and communicate information security risks
  - Focus on the **CIA Triad**:
    - Confidentiality
    - Integrity
    - Availability
  - Difference between Information Risk Management and Information Security

- **Importance of IRM**
  - Creating a safer working environment
  - Reducing organizational risks
  - Supporting project success
  - Financial benefits
  - Better decision-making
  - Protecting organizational reputation

- **Purpose of IRM**
  - Asset documentation
  - Threat monitoring
  - Defining incident response roles
  - Ensuring Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA)

- **IRM Process — 5 Steps**
  1. Identify potential points of vulnerability
  2. Analyze the types of data involved
  3. Evaluate and prioritize risks
     - `Risk Level = Likelihood × Financial Impact`
  4. Establish risk tolerance
     - Accept
     - Transfer
     - Mitigate
     - Reject
  5. Continuously monitor and review risks

- **IRM Best Practices**
  - Understand the IT environment and organizational assets
  - Build a robust risk management strategy
  - Embed risk management into organizational culture
  - Conduct continuous and adaptive risk assessments
  - Enforce security protocols
    - Firewalls
    - Patching
    - Access control
  - Maintain real-time visibility of risks

- **IRM Framework**
  - Relationship between Information Risk Management and the broader Risk Management Framework (RMF)
  - Tailoring risk management to organizational, data, and regulatory requirements

- **Benefits of an IRM Framework**
  - Competitive advantage and increased trust
  - Reduced likelihood of security incidents
  - Better business decision-making
  - Cost savings
  - Improved business continuity
  - Greater visibility into organizational risks
